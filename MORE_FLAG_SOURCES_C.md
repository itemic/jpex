# Seventeen more collections (batch C): flag sources

Retrieved 8 October 2026. All artwork is bundled for offline use. No flag was drawn, recoloured or edited for the app.

## Catalog scope

Every collection uses ISO 3166-2 codes as permanent IDs (`<country>-<code>`), with the code suffix as the abbreviation and the national flag (`world_flag_<cc>`) as the collection flag. Group IDs are `<country>-<slug>` and are display data only. The code lists were checked against the change history of each ISO 3166-2 entry (ISO Online Browsing Platform, as summarised on English Wikipedia); all 17 match the expected counts.

Differences from current law, and judgement calls:

- **Algeria.** Law 26-06 (promulgated April 2026) raised eleven wilaya-delegate districts to full wilayas (Aflou, Barika, El Kantara, Bir El Ater, El Aricha, Ksar Chellala, Aïn Oussera, Messaad, Ksar El Boukhari, Bou Saâda, El Abiodh Sidi Cheikh), so Algeria now has 69 wilayas. ISO 3166-2:DZ has not yet added them (its last change, 2022-11-29, added `DZ-49` to `DZ-58`), and neither has Wikidata. Following ISO, the collection lists 58; the new wilayas' territory is still inside the wilayas they were carved from. When ISO publishes the new codes, eleven places will need to be added.
- **Algeria grouping.** There are no administrative regions; the groups follow the planning areas (espaces de programmation territoriale) of the national land-use plan (SNAT), as used for the 2012–13 MICS survey: North Central (10), North East (8), North West (7), Highlands (the three Hauts-Plateaux areas, 14) and South (the Sud-Est, Sud-Ouest and Grand Sud areas, 9, plus the ten wilayas created in 2019, all carved from southern wilayas).
- **Tunisia grouping.** The seven regions used by the national statistics office (INS), with Greater Tunis separate from the North East. The five constitutional districts (aqalim) created by Decree 2023-589 group governorates differently (for example, Siliana with Sousse and Kairouan) and are not used.
- **Dominican Republic grouping.** The three macro-regions are used rather than the ten ISO development regions, so that the National District, which ISO places in Ozama with Santo Domingo Province, can stand on its own.
- **Paraguay.** Departments are listed in their official (Roman-numeral) order; Boquerón comes before Alto Paraguay, matching the current numbering (XVI, XVII), not ISO's codes.

## Names

English names follow English Wikipedia without type words (Anzoátegui, Alto Paraná, Chiriquí, Akwa Ibom, Greater Accra), and keep the Spanish diacritics. Exceptions where English usage differs: Capital District and Federal Dependencies (Venezuela), National District (Dominican Republic), Bay Islands (Honduras, `HN-IB`), Federal Capital Territory (Nigeria), Algiers (`DZ-16`), and Rwanda's Eastern/Northern/Western/Southern Province and Kigali. Tanzania uses Pwani for `TZ-19` (ISO's English "Coast") and Pemba North, Pemba South, Unguja North, Unguja South and Urban West for the Zanzibar regions. Namibia's `NA-KA` is written ǁKaras, as in the region's official name. Tunisian and Algerian names follow English Wikipedia (Kef, Kebili, Medenine, Naâma, M'Sila, El M'Ghair).

Local names are stored only where they differ from the English name: Spanish for Venezuela's Distrito Capital and Dependencias Federales, the Dominican Distrito Nacional and Honduras's Islas de la Bahía; Swahili for the Zanzibar regions (Kaskazini Pemba, Mjini Magharibi); Kinyarwanda for Rwanda (Umujyi wa Kigali, Intara y'Iburasirazuba); and Arabic for every Tunisian governorate and Algerian wilaya, without the word for governorate or province (تونس، القيروان، الجزائر، تمنراست). Group local names are given where the local term differs (Estados, Región Oriental, Departamentos, Provincias, Comarcas indígenas, Sureste, Tanzania Bara, Régions, Intara, and Arabic region names). Country local names: Panamá, República Dominicana, Sénégal, تونس, الجزائر.

## How flags were chosen

1. Each ISO code was matched to its Wikidata item through the MediaWiki API (`haswbstatement:P300=<code>`; all 363 codes matched, Anambra twice). The flag (P41) was read from the preferred statement, or else from a normal statement without an end date; deprecated statements were ignored.
2. These were compared with the flags in the English and Spanish (or Arabic, French, Swahili) Wikipedia infoboxes and with the Commons categories of subdivision flags. Status was then checked country by country against Flags of the World (FOTW), official gazettes and ordinances, government websites and news reports. A flag is shown only if the subdivision's own government adopted it, or clearly uses it as its flag. Flags of capital cities, sports and fan designs, proposals and reconstructions from coats of arms are not shown.
3. The artwork had to show the current design. Where it does not, or where it has the wrong colours or stripe order, the national flag is shown with a note. Every chosen flag was inspected on contact sheets, before and after conversion, and compared with FOTW's drawings where they exist.

Each flag is Wikimedia Commons' PNG render of the SVG at 960 pixels wide, resized to 768 pixels on the long side and reduced to a 256-colour palette. The alpha channel is kept whenever a render is not fully opaque; in this batch that means anti-aliased edges and stripe seams only. Three renders (Falcón, Zulia, Lagos) had one fully transparent row left at the bottom edge by thumbnail rounding (for example 960×481 from a 2:1 SVG); that empty row was trimmed. Fourteen of the chosen files are raster images (7 Paraguayan, 6 Salvadoran, 1 Panamanian) and were converted the same way from the original file. Five of them are small (Itapúa 324 px; Amambay 368 px; Cuscatlán, San Miguel and La Unión 363 px; La Paz 374 px) and were not enlarged.

Every file's author, source page and licence ship in `VenezuelaFlagCredits.json`, `ParaguayFlagCredits.json`, `GuatemalaFlagCredits.json`, `CostaRicaFlagCredits.json`, `PanamaFlagCredits.json`, `ElSalvadorFlagCredits.json` and `NigeriaFlagCredits.json`. The Dominican Republic, Jamaica, Honduras, Tanzania, Ghana, Senegal, Rwanda, Namibia, Tunisia and Algeria use only their national flags, which are already credited in `WorldFlagCredits.json`.

Licences: 67 files. Public domain 28, CC0 8, CC BY-SA 4.0 19, CC BY-SA 3.0 11, CC BY-SA 2.5 1. The CC BY-SA files require attribution, which the credits files give. Seven files carry both a public-domain tag (official-symbol or simple-shape) and the uploader's own CC BY-SA 4.0 licence (Capital District, the four Guatemalan flags, Panamá Oeste, Akwa Ibom); they are credited under CC BY-SA 4.0.

## Notes by collection

- **Venezuela (24 of 25).** All 23 states have flags set by governor's decree, legislative-council act or state symbols law since 1991, several replaced since: Lara (2000), Sucre (15 stars, 2002), Falcón (2006, based on Miranda's 1806 flag), Miranda (2006) and Carabobo (1995 design, reinstated in 2008). Anzoátegui shows the flag of Decree 138 (1999) without the arms; Zulia shows the flag of Decree 231 (1991), not the government variant with arms. Delta Amacuro's only SVG is the government rendering with the state arms near the hoist, which the 2004 law does not describe; it is shown with a credit note. The Capital District has no legislature of its own and is coextensive with Libertador Municipality; it shows the Caracas flag adopted by the Libertador council on 13 April 2022, which the national statistics office presents as the Distrito Capital's flag. The Federal Dependencies have no official flag (the Commons design is marked as alleged on Spanish Wikipedia).
- **Paraguay (12 of 18).** Departmental governments (since 1992) adopt symbols by ordinance, but documentation is thin. Shown: Asunción (municipal decree 4635 of 1961, arms of Ordinance 208/2001; Asunción is itself the first-level unit), Central (Ordinance 4/99), Ñeembucú (Ordinance 02/2020; the older white–teal–blue files are superseded), Itapúa (Ordinance 10/94), Paraguarí (2009), and, on weaker evidence of official use (FOTW from departmental websites, Spanish Wikipedia), Concepción, Cordillera, Guairá, Caaguazú, Misiones, Amambay and Boquerón. Where Wikidata's recent SVGs leave out the departmental arms that the real flags carry (Concepción, Cordillera, Itapúa, Misiones, Paraguarí, Amambay), raster files with the arms were chosen. Concepción's diagonal follows FOTW's description (tierced per bend sinister), not FOTW's mirrored drawing.
- **Guatemala (4 of 22).** Departments are run by appointed governors, and no law or council resolution adopting a departmental flag was found. Shown only where the departmental government confirmed or flew the flag: Chimaltenango, Retalhuleu and Jutiapa (governors' official letters of 1997–98, quoted on FOTW; current use unverified, weakest for Jutiapa) and Totonicapán (raised by the Gobernación on 12 November 2025, per the government news agency; the red–white–red flag, not Wikidata's older red–white–yellow file). The other departments' Commons flags are mostly unsourced 2025 drawings, capital-city flags (Guatemala, Huehuetenango, Sololá, Quetzaltenango), superseded designs (Baja Verapaz), wrong stripe orders (Petén, San Marcos) or designs that change with governors (Zacapa). Chiquimula's 2012 flag came from a contest run by a television programme and is not shown.
- **Costa Rica (4 of 7).** Provinces have no government and no law sets provincial flags. Shown as established flags chosen in public selections and in civic use: Guanacaste (1974), Limón (1992), Heredia (1993) and Puntarenas (1994). Limón's long-standing Commons files are upside down; the file used has the order white, light blue, green given by the Municipality of Limón, FOTW and English Wikipedia (the same design serves the capital canton). San José never adopted a flag (the Commons file is a proposal), and the Alajuela and Cartago designs have no documented adoption.
- **Panama (8 of 14).** There is no general law on provincial flags; flags are adopted by the provincial councils. Shown: Veraguas (Resolution 12 of 2019, Gaceta Oficial 28772-A), Chiriquí (1995; the file has 14 stars for today's 14 districts), Herrera (Resolution 29 of 2012 and Decree 1 of 2013), Coclé (2004), Panamá Oeste (2015), Colón (1996), Guna Yala (adopted by the Guna General Congress in 2010) and Naso Tjër Di (flown at the comarca's creation in December 2020; drawn after that photograph).
- **El Salvador (12 of 14).** The departmental flags (most created in the 1940s–60s) were confirmed by all 14 governors to Fred Drews in 1997–99 and drawn for FOTW; Chalatenango (1969) and Usulután (1965) have documented proclamations. The chosen files match FOTW's drawings; four are small copies of them (Cuscatlán, La Paz, La Unión, San Miguel). Morazán's only good file shows the 2005 flag ("DIOS UNION PROGRESO"), while FOTW's current image (2014) reads "DIOS UNION Y LIBERTAD" and "14 DE JULIO DE 1875"; Usulután's green–white–blue flag appears on Commons only with a purple stripe or wrong stars. Both show the national flag. An untitled 2026 Morazán upload (a plain tricolour) was rejected.
- **Nigeria (3 of 37).** Shown: Akwa Ibom (unveiled September 2020), Bayelsa (Flag, Coat of Arms and Anthem Law 2012) and Lagos (flown by the state government; a 2024 government report of the flags at the Alausa Secretariat). Commons has many sports-festival banners (Enugu, Kano, Sokoto, Rivers, Kogi, Benue), logos on white, seals and fan designs for the other states; they are not shown. Adamawa's file is the Cameroonian Adamaoua region's, and Katsina's is the emirate's. Flags exist without accurate artwork for Cross River (blue–white–blue, but the only file uses a dark violet blue), Ogun (only a blurred 308-pixel video still) and Oyo (only an incorrect reconstruction); Abia's, Anambra's and Delta's current flags are uncertain, and Nasarawa's approved flag is undocumented. Osun's state assembly repealed its 2012 flag law in July 2023. The FCT has only the Abuja city logo banner.
- **Namibia.** FOTW reports flags of the Erongo, Khomas, ǁKaras and Kunene regional councils, seen in use in 2022–24, but no freely licensed artwork exists for any of them, and no adoption was found. All regions show the national flag.
- **Ghana.** Regional coordinating councils are deconcentrated bodies with no flags. The Commons "regional flags" for Greater Accra, Eastern, Western and Volta are an unsourced 2014 batch (Commons tags several as fictitious), and "Flag of Ashanti" is the Asante kingdom's flag, not the region's.
- **Tunisia.** The four "governorate flags" on English Wikipedia (Sousse, Mahdia, Gafsa, Gabès) are 2025–26 uploads built from municipal logos or a logo on an invented field; FOTW lists only municipal flags.
- **Jamaica, Honduras, Dominican Republic, Tanzania, Senegal, Rwanda, Algeria.** No official subdivision flags. Jamaica: the Hanover and Kingston and St Andrew municipal corporations fly seal flags, which are not parish flags and have no artwork. Honduras: departments have no self-government; the Commons designs are fan-made or capital-city flags (Tegucigalpa for Francisco Morazán; Valle's file belongs to Valle de Ángeles, a municipality elsewhere). Dominican Republic: governors are appointed and no province has adopted a flag; the Commons files for Santiago (city arms), Puerto Plata, San Pedro de Macorís, Independencia and Monseñor Nouel are unsourced, and the National District's council banner is the city's. Tanzania: the flags FOTW lists for Arusha, Dar es Salaam, Dodoma and Mwanza belong to city councils, and Zanzibar's flag is not a region's. Senegal: regional councils were abolished in 2013. Algeria: wilayas use logos, mostly generic since 2019.
